Things to Know

Money · shopping · spa secrets · jet lag · manners · the rules of the table

💵Money

Korea — the won. Roughly ₩1,400 to the dollar, so a ₩100,000 dinner is about $70. Cards work absolutely everywhere — Korea is nearly cashless — but carry some won: the Heesum tea ceremony (₩160,000 each, ₩640,000 for the four of you) is settled on-site, and cash is warmly appreciated there.
Thailand — the baht. About ฿35 to the dollar; a ฿1,000 dinner is $28. Cards at hotels and restaurants; cash for markets, tuk-tuk drivers and small spots. Thai ATMs add a ~฿220 fee per withdrawal — take out more, less often.
Skip the airport exchange counters; the hotel ATMs give better rates. Rates drift, so check the week we leave.

🛍Tax-free shopping

Korea refunds its 10% VAT to tourists — and with a Cheongdam afternoon on the calendar, this is real money. In stores marked "Tax Free," show your passport at the register: smaller purchases get the tax knocked off instantly, on the spot.
For bigger purchases, keep the refund slips — there are self-service kiosks at Incheon (before security, Dec 24): scan passport, scan slips, done in minutes.
Thailand refunds 7% at stores displaying "VAT Refund for Tourists" (minimum ฿2,000 per store, per day): ask for the yellow P.P.10 form with your receipt, then visit the VAT counter at Phuket airport before check-in on Jan 3 — with the goods in hand.

📦Shipping the finds home

Chatuchak is built for this. If a lamp, a pair of ceramic vessels, or a carved console steals your heart, shipping agents inside the market pack and freight anything, any size — your guide will steer you to the reliable ones. Sea freight for big pieces runs 4–8 weeks to New Jersey; air is faster and pricier.
In Seoul, the concierge is already shipping your winter clothes home on Dec 24 — the same channel happily takes anything you buy in Cheongdam.
Keep receipts together for US customs; household goods for personal use clear without drama.

🤝Tipping

Korea: don't. Truly. Tipping isn't part of the culture and can even cause a flustered chase down the hallway to return your money. Service is superb without it.
Thailand: modestly, happily. ฿300–500 for a full-day guide or boat crew, ฿100–200 for drivers, round up at restaurants (many already add 10% service), ฿50–100 a day for villa staff at Trisara — and the NYE gala staff will have earned it.

🧖The Korean spa, decoded

Day 1 opens at the Four Seasons spa, so here's what nobody tells you: Korean bathhouse floors are separated by gender, and swimsuit-free — everyone's doing it, nobody's looking, and after 16 hours of flying you will not care. Your actual treatments happen in private rooms, professionally draped, couples' rooms as booked.
The famous seshin scrub is vigorous — closer to being lovingly sanded than massaged — and you emerge with the softest skin of your adult life. Drink water before and after, arrive 30 minutes early for the heated pools, and leave the phone in the room.

😴Beating the 14-hour flip

Seoul is 14 hours ahead — the hardest time change there is, and the itinerary is quietly built as the cure. You land 6:30am; the mission on Day 1 is stay awake until 10pm, no real naps. The spa keeps you moving without demanding anything, daylight resets your clock, and the tea ceremony arrives exactly at the 4pm energy dip on purpose.
If you use melatonin, the first two nights are the ones. By Day 2's T.um visit you'll feel human; by Day 3, local.
Coming home you land at JFK 8:15am Monday — same trick in reverse: push through to 9pm, and Tuesday feels normal.

📱Phones & apps

In Korea, Google Maps barely works (mapping data is restricted by law) — download Naver Map and switch it to English before you land; it's excellent. Papago beats Google Translate for Korean, and reads menus through the camera.
In Thailand, Grab is the Uber — though with drivers arranged for both Bangkok days, you may never open it.
Set up an eSIM or international plan before JFK; both Four Seasons and Trisara have flawless Wi-Fi.

💬Eight words that matter

Korea: An-nyeong-ha-se-yo (hello) · Gam-sa-ham-ni-da (thank you) · Ju-se-yo (please) · Ma-shi-sseo-yo (delicious — say it and watch the kitchen light up).
Thailand: Sa-wat-dee kha (hello — men end with khrap) · Khop khun kha/khrap (thank you) · Mai phet (NOT spicy — the four most important syllables of the Thai leg) · A-roi (delicious).

🙏Manners in two kingdoms

Korea: give and receive with two hands — cards, cash, gifts, a glass being filled. At dinner, pour for others, never for yourself. Shoes off wherever you see them lined up at the door.
Thailand: return a wai (the pressed-palms bow) with a smile and a nod. The head is sacred and the feet are lowly — never point feet at a Buddha image, and step over the raised temple thresholds at Wat Arun, not on them. Women don't hand things directly to monks.
One serious one: Thailand's monarchy is protected by law — even joking about the royal family is a crime. Simply not a topic.

🕰Time & calling home

Seoul: 14 hours ahead of New York. Thailand: 12 hours ahead — delightfully simple, just swap AM and PM.
Best call windows: 8am from Phuket is 8pm the night before in New Jersey; their 7am is your 7pm. Anyone who texts you at your midnight gets answered at their midnight — fair is fair.

🛂Entry & documents

Passports valid 6+ months past Jan 3, 2027 — check all four now, renewals take weeks.
Korea: US travelers may need a K-ETA (a quick online approval, ~$7) — the requirement has been waived and reinstated over the years, so we'll verify and file all four before travel.
Thailand: currently visa-free for US visitors on stays like ours; there's now a simple online arrival card to file shortly before landing — we'll handle it that week. Dubai on the way home is transit-only; nothing needed.

🍽How we eat

Every restaurant on this trip was chosen around the table's rules: gluten-free for Marshall · no meat, poultry or shellfish for anyone · no raw fish for Susan.
That means fin fish and vegetable-forward menus start to finish — and the guides, boat crews and concierges are briefed ahead, so ordering is never a negotiation. In Korea, watch for hidden wheat (soy sauce, gochujang) with Marshall — Papago's camera on the menu plus a briefed server covers it.

🏥Health, water & emergencies

Seoul: tap water is safe. Dial 1330 — Korea's 24/7 English tourist hotline, genuinely useful for anything — or 119 for medical. World-class hospitals minutes from the hotel.
Thailand: bottled water only (the hotels stock endless supplies); ice at our hotels and restaurants is fine. Medical emergency 1669, tourist police 1155. Bumrungrad in Bangkok and Bangkok Hospital Phuket are among Asia's best — and both hotels summon an English-speaking doctor to your room 24/7.
Pack: sunscreen, insect repellent for the jungle dinner at Three Monkeys, motion-sickness tabs if anyone's prone (four boat days), and any prescriptions in original bottles.

✈Quiet by design

No Saturday flights — the whole route was drawn that way from the start.
Winter clothes ship home from Seoul Dec 24, so the tropical leg travels light.
VIP airport service at every friction point — landing at Incheon, check-in at Incheon, arrival in Bangkok, departure from Phuket. Nobody stands in a line on this trip.

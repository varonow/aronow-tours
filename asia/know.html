The Practical Page

Things to Know

Money · manners · boat days · jet lag · the rules of the table · and everything nobody thinks to mention until you need it

💵Money

Korea — the won. About ₩1,400 to the dollar. A ₩100,000 dinner is $70; a ₩10,000 coffee is a very reasonable $7. Cards work everywhere — Korea is borderline cashless — but bring some won in your wallet: the Heesum tea ceremony (₩160,000 per person, settled on-site) warmly prefers it, and cash makes the market easier.
Thailand — the baht. About ฿35 to the dollar, which makes everything feel wonderfully affordable. Cards at hotels and nice restaurants; cash for markets, tuk-tuk drivers, and anywhere without a sign. Thai ATMs charge a flat ~฿220 fee regardless of what you take out — withdraw more, withdraw less often.
Skip the airport exchange booths entirely. They're convenient and terrible. The hotel ATM is right there.

🛍The government is giving you money back

Korea refunds 10% VAT to tourists — which on a Cheongdam afternoon of skincare and cashmere is not a small number. At any store with a "Tax Free" sign, show your passport at the register. For most purchases the tax comes off on the spot. For larger ones, keep the refund slips and use the self-service kiosks at Incheon before security on Dec 24. Scan passport, scan slips, collect. It takes four minutes.
Thailand gives back 7% at stores marked "VAT Refund for Tourists" (minimum ฿2,000 per store). Ask for the yellow P.P.10 form, keep your receipts together, and stop at the VAT refund counter at Phuket airport on Jan 3 — before check-in, goods in bag. Slightly bureaucratic, genuinely worth it.

📦When you buy something enormous

Chatuchak is built for this. If a lamp, a pair of ceramic vessels, or a carved console from section 3 suddenly feels non-negotiable, don't panic about how to get it home. Shipping agents are embedded throughout the market and they pack and freight anything — your guide will point you to the reliable ones. Sea freight to New Jersey takes 4–8 weeks; air is faster and pricier. Both are fine.
Seoul: the concierge is already handling the luggage ship-back on Dec 24 (one bag to Deal, one to Brooklyn), so anything from Cheongdam can travel the same route. Same service, different box.
Keep your receipts in one place for US customs. Personal household goods clear without drama.

🤝Tipping

Korea: genuinely don't. It's not customary, it can cause confusion, and in some situations the staff will actually chase you down the hallway to give it back. The service here is exceptional and tipping isn't part of why. Just enjoy it.
Thailand: yes, warmly. ฿300–500 for a full-day guide or boat crew at the end of each charter (this matters to them — four boat days means four crews), ฿100–200 for drivers, round up at restaurants, ฿50–100 a day for the Trisara villa staff. The NYE gala team especially will have earned it.

🧖The Signiel Spa — Day 1's secret weapon

Floors 76 and above in Lotte World Tower, partnered with Swiss and French skincare houses, and the views alone are worth the trip up. This is not a hotel spa that happens to exist — it is the destination. Two couples' rooms means all four of us treated at the same time: we go in together, we come out together, and The Lounge for lunch is one elevator ride away.
Tell them you're recovering from a 16-hour flight. Post-flight recovery is a known specialty and they will calibrate the treatment accordingly. Arrive a few minutes early, leave the phones in the locker, and let Seoul appear below you through the floor-to-ceiling glass while someone takes care of the flight out of your shoulders.
Drink water before. Leave feeling entirely different than you arrived.

😴Beating the 14-hour flip

Seoul is 14 hours ahead — the hardest time change there is — and the Day 1 itinerary is quietly engineered as the cure. The trick is simple and brutal: stay awake until 10pm. No real naps. The spa keeps you moving without demanding energy, the tower and Seoul Sky keep your eyes open, and the tea ceremony arrives at exactly 5pm — right when the jet lag usually wins.
Melatonin helps. The first two nights are the ones that matter. By the T.um visit on Day 2 you'll feel like a person; by Day 3 you'll feel local.
Coming home: you land JFK at 8:15am Monday. Same trick in reverse — push through to 9pm that night, and Tuesday is normal. Emirates First makes the overnight flight genuinely restful, which helps.

🛺The tuk-tuk — what it's actually like

Two private tuk-tuks, two couples, the open streets of old Bangkok. You are going to be loud and you are going to love it. A tuk-tuk is not designed for conversation — the engine is right there — but it is designed for exactly this: moving through the city at speed with the warm night air on your face and nowhere in particular to be except the next spectacular thing.
The flower market arrives first. Pak Khlong Talat at night is stacked floor-to-ceiling with orchids, jasmine, marigold garlands and lotus in colors that have no business being that vivid at 7pm. Then into Yaowarat — Chinatown at full volume, neon signs in Thai and Chinese, street food carts and the energy of a city that never really quiets down.
Hold on. Keep phones in pockets through the market. Don't worry about where you're going — the driver knows. Just look.

🛍Chatuchak — how to do it right

Fifteen thousand stalls across 35 acres. Your guide is briefed specifically on the design, antiques and home sections — ceramics, lamps, vintage textiles, beautiful objects that have no business being this affordable. You will not be wandering; you'll be taken directly to the good stuff.
The heat is real even in December. The itinerary moves lunch earlier specifically to build in a cool-down before the afternoon session. Hydrate continuously. The guide knows where the good cold coconuts are.
On negotiating: prices are marked but almost everything moves. A friendly counter at 70–80% of asking is normal and expected. Don't lowball aggressively — it's bad form and the guide will read when a price is already fair. For serious pieces, let the guide lead; they know which vendors pack reliably.
If something is too big to carry home: shipping agents are embedded throughout the market. Get the business card, confirm they take cards, keep the receipt. Done.

⛵Four boat days — the briefing

Dec 28 Phang Nga Bay · Dec 30 Racha Islands · Dec 31 morning fishing · Jan 1 Phi Phi. Every boat is private, every captain speaks English, every lunch is catered to the table's rules. Here's what else to know.
What a hong actually is: "hong" means room in Thai. They are hidden lagoons completely enclosed by limestone cliffs, and the only way in is by kayak through a low sea cave — your guide paddling, you ducking — until the world opens into a secret garden of green water and vertical rock with birds and monitor lizards and silence. The captain times it around the tides. You don't need experience. You do need to duck.
For Susan: every boat has shaded deck seating and the view from anchor at Pileh Lagoon is as extraordinary as the view from the water. Nobody needs to get wet unless they want to. The crew will make sure of it.
Reef-safe sunscreen only at Racha and Phi Phi — it's a Thai national park rule and a good one. Standard sunscreen chemicals damage coral. Hats and a long-sleeve rash guard are worth it for a full day at sea in December.
Motion sickness: if anyone is prone, the behind-the-ear patch (Scopolamine) is the most effective for full-day use. Put it on the night before — not the morning of, not when you're already queasy.
Tip the crew at the end of each charter day: ฿300–500 each. Four boat days, four crews. It matters to them.

🌴Trisara — what to expect

The name is Sanskrit for "The Garden in the Third Heaven." It lives up to it. Thirty-nine private pool villas down a hillside to a private cove, Michelin Guide One Key, two Michelin-starred restaurants, and a staff that has been remembering guests' names and coffee orders for twenty years. This is where the trip shifts from exciting to genuinely restorative.
The villas: private infinity pool, Andaman views, no neighbors visible. Everything in the minibar is included. The bathroom is almost as big as the bedroom. If you want a private dinner on Jan 1 — shoes off, your music, fish and Thai dishes on the terrace with the sea below — just tell the Synergist Team when you arrive. They set the whole thing up.
The beach is genuinely private — the only one in Phuket. It's intimate rather than sprawling, with flat stones at the waterline (built for boat access to the pier). For a long walk, wide Nai Thon Beach is right next door and rarely crowded.
The service: the Trisara philosophy is "invisible service" — things happen before you think to ask. You will not feel followed. You will simply find things done. If anything isn't right, say so; they're genuinely there to fix it.
Getting around Phuket: Trisara arranges all your transfers — for activities, dinners, the airport. Phuket is spread out and the roads are winding; let them handle it. That's what they're there for.

🎆New Year's Eve — what to wear

The dress code is Jewel Elegance in the Garden. Translation: your most beautiful. Rich colors, jewel tones, something that feels like a real occasion. This is the night for the silk dress, the statement earrings, the thing you almost left at home.
Phuket in December evenings is around 26°C with a sea breeze — warm enough that heavy fabric will be uncomfortable by the cabaret. Lightweight fabrics in deep colors are exactly right. Flat sandals or low heels on a terrace; the path from your villa to The Deck takes about thirty seconds.
And then: dinner over the Andaman, live jazz, a cabaret show, fireworks at midnight, and the shortest walk home of any New Year's Eve you've ever had. No car, no midnight panic, no freezing. Just the sea and the four of you.

🌿Three Monkeys — the jungle dinner

Built into the hillside inside Hanuman World, on wooden platforms among actual trees, with Phuket glittering below and a private dome for the four of us. A live saxophonist. No neighboring tables. It is as theatrical as it sounds and it earns every bit of it.
A practical note: it's outdoors in the tropics at night, which means insects are part of the evening. Bring repellent — it matters more here than anywhere else on the trip. The pathways are lit but not floodlit, so closed shoes or sandals with straps.
The menu is wide and wonderful — grilled fish, Southern Thai, Japanese, wood-fired everything, cocktails. Order generously and share. The kitchen has the full dietary brief. This is an evening you'll still be talking about on the flight home.

📱Phones & apps

Download Naver Map before you land in Korea. Google Maps is legally restricted there and barely functions — Naver works beautifully in English and your guide will use it too. Papago is the translation app to have: it beats Google Translate for Korean and has a camera function that reads menus in real time, which is the most useful thing on earth at 7pm with jet lag.
In Thailand, Grab is the app for taxis — though with drivers arranged for both Bangkok days and Trisara handling Phuket, you may never need to open it.
Sort out an international plan or eSIM before JFK. Both Four Seasons properties and Trisara have excellent Wi-Fi, but having data on you feels better.

💬Words that actually matter

Korea — the essentials:
An-nyeong-ha-se-yo — hello
Gam-sa-ham-ni-da — thank you
Ju-se-yo — please / I'd like this
Ma-shi-sseo-yo — delicious (say this and watch the kitchen light up from across the room)
Kadeu-ro gyeol-je-hae-ju-se-yo — please charge my credit card
Ga-ge-neun eo-di-e it-na-yo? — where are the shops?
Ma-ti-ni han-jan ju-se-yo — I'd like a martini, please
Thailand — the essentials:
Sa-wat-dee kha / khrap — hello (women say kha, men say khrap — applies to everything)
Khop khun kha / khrap — thank you
Mai phet — not spicy (possibly the four most important syllables of the whole trip)
A-roi — delicious (use liberally and mean it)
Chep bat-kred dai mai kha? — can I pay by credit card?
Ran-kha yoo tee-nai kha? — where are the shops?
Kho mar-ti-ni nueng kaew kha — one martini, please (the most important sentence in Thailand)
Pro tip: end every sentence with kha (women) or khrap (men) and you will be received warmly everywhere. It costs nothing and means everything.

🙏Manners in two kingdoms

Korea: give and receive with two hands — cards, cash, gifts, a glass being refilled. Pour for everyone else at dinner; never pour for yourself. Shoes come off when you see them lined up at the door. The whole culture runs on mutual consideration and it's lovely once you're in it.
Thailand: return the wai (pressed palms, slight bow) with a smile and a nod — you don't need to initiate it, just return it warmly. The head is sacred, the feet are the opposite: never point feet at a Buddha image. Step over the raised threshold at Wat Arun, not on it. Women don't hand things directly to monks.
The one serious rule: Thailand's royal family is protected by strict law. Not a topic, not a joke, not a comment. Simply not there.

🕰Time & calling home

Seoul is 14 hours ahead of New York. Thailand is 12 hours ahead — which is delightfully simple math: just swap AM and PM. 8am in Phuket is 8pm the night before in New Jersey.
The practical upshot: morning calls from the villas work perfectly for anyone back home who's still up. Anyone who texts at your midnight gets answered at their midnight. Fair is fair.

🛂Documents — do this before we leave

Check all four passports now — they need to be valid at least 6 months past January 3, 2027. Renewals take longer than you think.
Korea K-ETA: the requirement has been waived and reinstated over the years, so check the current status before travel and file all four online if needed. It takes five minutes and costs about $7 per person. Don't leave it until the week of departure.
Thailand: US visitors are currently visa-free for our length of stay. There's a simple online arrival card to file shortly before landing — we'll handle it together that week. Dubai is transit-only on the way home; nothing needed there.

🍽How we eat

Every restaurant on this trip was chosen with the table's rules in mind: no meat, no poultry, no shellfish for anyone · gluten-free for Marshall · no raw fish for Susan.
That's the promise: fin fish and vegetable-forward menus from Seoul to Phuket, and every guide, concierge, boat crew and restaurant has been briefed in advance. Ordering is never a negotiation at the table. It's already handled before we sit down.
Korea has a few hidden wheat moments — fermented pastes and some sauces occasionally contain it. Papago's camera on the menu plus a briefed server covers Marshall at every meal. When in doubt, ask; Korean hospitality takes this seriously.

🏥Health & emergencies — just in case

Seoul: tap water is safe. For anything from lost keys to a medical question, dial 1330 — Korea's 24/7 English tourist hotline and genuinely one of the most useful phone numbers in the world. Medical emergency is 119. World-class hospitals are minutes from the hotel.
Thailand: bottled water only (hotels provide endless supplies; ice at the hotels and restaurants is fine). Medical emergency: 1669. Tourist police: 1155. Bumrungrad International in Bangkok and Bangkok Hospital Phuket are both outstanding — and both Four Seasons properties can summon an English-speaking doctor to the room around the clock.
For the boat days: reef cuts heal slowly in the tropics — rinse any scrape in fresh water and clean it properly. And if anyone tends toward motion sickness, the behind-the-ear patch (Scopolamine) is the thing. Apply it the night before.

✈Quiet by design

The whole route was built around a set of values, not just connections. No flights on the day of rest. Winter clothes shipped home from Seoul so the tropical leg travels light. VIP service at every airport friction point — someone waiting at the gate or the exit every single time.
At Incheon at 6:30am, two VIP agents walk us through immigration. In Bangkok on Christmas Eve, the Four Seasons Fast Track team meets us off the plane. On Dec 27, Bangkok Fast Track handles the domestic departure to Phuket. At Phuket, Trisara's rep is at Exit Gate 1 with a sign. And Emirates First takes care of everything on the way home.
Nobody stands in a line on this trip. The days built with nothing scheduled — the afternoon on Dec 27, the whole of Jan 2 — are not oversights. They are the point. Two weeks at this pace needs space to breathe, and it has it.
